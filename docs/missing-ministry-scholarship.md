# Missing Ministry: What the Local Shelf Gives, and What It Still Lacks

## Abstract

This literature review surveys the local shelf (`research/scholarship-private/`, surveyed 2026-09-24) for the temple argument behind `docs/missing-ministry-temple-essay.md`. Its thesis is that the shelf already supplies roughly eighty percent of an ancient-roots *background* brief — veil, priesthood, Eden-temple, and garment idiom — but none of the forty-day brief itself: 80% of the scenery, 0% of the plot. Its method is strictly local: no web research is used here, and the published-literature survey is still pending. Its finding is a fetch list and a citation plan for the essay's next pass.

## Introduction

The problem is practical: the temple essay needs citable ancient-roots support without overclaiming what the local shelf holds. The question this review answers is what the shelf already gives, which nearby documents already argue adjacent theses, and where the gaps force new fetching. Why it matters is that the essay's credibility turns on evenhandedness — showing its work, hedging its Barker-dependence, and marking inference as inference. The roadmap follows that order: holdings, neighbors, gaps, then consequences for the essay.

## 1. What the shelf already gives: toward an ancient-roots brief

The shelf's center of gravity is Margaret Barker, the Methodist scholar of First-Temple religion whose reconstruction the review uses as comparative lens rather than authority. Four Barker files each earn their place.

### Barker on the veil (`barker-veil.txt`)

This file argues the veil is matter or firmament itself, entered by the high priest once yearly, with apocalypses read as holy-of-holies visions (Revelation 4:1 "come up hither," 1 Enoch 14, 3 Enoch veil passages). What that says is that apocalyptic ascent is temple entry in literary form. Why it matters here is direct ascent-and-veil theology for the essay's Philip and Pistis rows.

### Barker on the high priest (`barker-highpriest.txt`)

This file treats anointing oil and the tree of life, Melchizedek priest-kings, and the Day of Atonement blood ritual. What that contributes is the anointing complex. Why it matters is that it stands closest on the shelf to the essay's endowment-anointing row.

### Barker on Josiah (`barker-josiah.txt`)

This file narrates hidden ark, oil, manna, and rod, with Asherah and menorah removal and a Melchizedek plus 11Q13 connection. What that argument says is that a reform lost things a restoration would need to restore. Why it matters is framing — the review recommends it for the essay's introduction as the "what was lost that restoration restores" frame.

### Barker on wisdom and the tree (`barker-wisdom-tree.txt`)

This file reads Eden itself as temple, organized around the two trees and the Lady or Wisdom figure. What that says is garden, temple, and creation drama belong to one row. Why it matters is the essay's garden-to-temple-to-creation-drama sequence.

### Bradshaw on the temple (`bradshaw-temple.txt`, about forty pages)

The fullest Latter-day Saint synthesis on the shelf. Bradshaw, an LDS scholar of temple and Enoch studies, treats Enoch ascent, veiling after ascent, Eden-as-temple, the Tree of Life in the Holy of Holies, and garments with covenant. What that says is that ascent, veiling, and robing form one complex. Why it matters is that it is the most explicit LDS endowment-parallel on the shelf — the review directs it to the essay's garment and veil rows.

### Bokovoy on Jacob (`bokovoy-jacob.html`)

Bokovoy, the LDS Hebrew Bible scholar, reads Jacob 2:2 as temple discourse with garments rid of sin. What that says is that the Book of Mormon itself models temple discourse. Why it matters is pairing: it gives the essay a Book-of-Mormon temple-discourse model alongside its Twelve-to-3-Nephi row.

### Christensen on paradigms (`christensen-paradigms.txt`)

This file applies a Kuhn-and-Barker apologetic frame to the question of using Barker at all. Who Kuhn is here matters: Thomas Kuhn, historian of science, whose paradigm account Christensen borrows to defend minority readings. Why it matters is method cover — permission to argue with Barker without pretending she is consensus.

### Hamblin on Josiah (`hamblin-josiah.html`)

Hamblin, as LDS historian of the ancient world, defends the Josiah reform as counterpoint. What that says is the reform need not be read as pure loss. Why it matters is hedge value: citing it shows evenhandedness and guards against Barker-dependence.

### Background, not ritual: Heiser, Charles, and the index

The remaining shelf items are background, not ritual.

The Heiser dissertation and shorts — Heiser being the Hebrew Bible scholar of the divine council — supply the divine-council and Deuteronomy 32 backdrop for enthronement and ascent settings. What that says is that ascent happens before a council, not in a void. The review's judgment is that this is setting, not rite.

Charles 1913 volume 2 (`apot-charles/`) supplies public-domain primaries — 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Ascension of Isaiah — behind Barker and Bradshaw. What that gives is citable primary text where the essay needs it.

Finally the symbol index (`symbol-index.json`, actually 711 entries — the README count is stale) tracks some of the ancient-roots vocabulary as proper symbols — veil 13, Holy of Holies 8, Melchizedek 7, all verified by count — but has no temple, anointing, garment, forty-day, or endowment symbols at all. What that says is mixed: the index follows ascent architecture (veil, Holiest, Melchizedek) while the rite-specific vocabulary the essay needs is unindexed. The gap is itself a finding, and the missing symbols belong on the index backlog in §4.

## 2. Local documents already arguing nearby theses

Four neighboring documents constrain what the essay must and must not redo. `docs/neighboring-witnesses.md` already covers Ascension of Isaiah robes (:29), the Sabbath Songs angel-choir leading to 3 Nephi 19 and D&C 109 (:31), the Odes presence-texts (:33), the 1QS washing-garment-song (:41) plus 11Q13 Jubilee-Atonement complex (:45), and the Hodayot (Thanksgiving Hymns) transformation idiom (:43); the same file's Wisdom survey (:47-55) already states the temple-presence lens with its Barker-boundary warning and its Philo Logos survey (:57-59) with the Platonist caution. What that means for the essay is that it can cite rather than rebuild those surveys. `docs/ascending-to-presence-article.md:58-130` already works the Baker-plus-Ricks Psalter libretto (Psalm 24 gate liturgy and related psalms), distinguishes Morales — L. Michael Morales, *Who Shall Ascend the Mountain of the Lord?* (2015), a Leviticus theology taking Psalm 24:3 as epigraph rather than a Psalter-arrangement argument — and maps stations — with a lens-not-fact discipline the review recommends imitating. `docs/christ-in-old-testament.md` already treats Leviticus 16 within-veil blood, Exodus 26 veil-and-flesh (Hebrews 10:19-20), Psalms 110 and 132, and the Isaiah 25 feast. What that leaves open is the review's key sentence: the forty-day ministry itself is never treated there — the temple essay fills that hole.

## 3. Gaps: scholars leaned on versus ignored, and what to fetch

The review distinguishes likely load-bearing scholars from absent ones. Likely leaned on: Barker on veil and priesthood, Baker plus Ricks on ascent, Bradshaw, Bokovoy, and Heiser on the council. Absent from the shelf entirely — verified citations, forty-day relevance each: Himmelfarb, Martha. *Ascent to Heaven in Jewish and Christian Apocalypses* (Oxford UP, 1993) — the ascent-theory control the shelf lacks. Elior, Rachel. *The Three Temples: On the Emergence of Jewish Mysticism* (Littman, 2004) — the temple-mysticism counterpart to Barker. Feeley-Harnik, Gillian. *The Lord's Table: Eucharist and Passover in Early Christianity* (Penn Press, 1981; expanded as *The Meaning of Food in Early Judaism and Christianity*, Smithsonian, 1994) — the meal theology the essay's unity row needs. Nibley, Hugh. *Temple and Cosmos*, CWHN vol. 12 (Deseret/FARMS, 1992) — the LDS temple-cosmos voice. Smith, Mark S. *The Early History of God* (2nd ed., Eerdmans, 2002) — the critical counterweight on Israelite religion. Goff, Matthew — no single Goff monograph covers ascent-temple; closest is *Discerning Wisdom* (Brill, 2007), with the ascent-specific slot better filled by Himmelfarb above. There is no forty-day or post-resurrection shelf entry at all — the shelf's central hole. (Jonah Barnes's own trilogy, the project that occasioned this review, is likewise absent from the shelf; claims about what Barnes depends on await the books in hand.)

Fetch targets, restricted to open-access or fair-use excerpts, one need per target, ordered by load-bearing absence first:

- Himmelfarb on ascent: the ascent-theory control the shelf lacks.
- Elior on temples: the temple-mysticism counterpart to Barker.
- Feeley-Harnik on meals: the meal theology the essay's unity row needs.
- Cameron, Perkins, Koester, Bockmuehl on the dialogue gospels: the critical control on dating and genre — the actual central hole, since the temple essay already cites their positions without the shelf holding them.
- Nibley's temple essays: the LDS temple-cosmos voice (lineage, not independent control — corroborates nothing, ranks accordingly).
- Goff and Heiser Watchers integration: the Enochic-priesthood bridge (peripheral to a forty-day thesis, ranks last).
- The forty-day and veil chapter excerpt of Barker's *Temple Theology*: the paywalled Barker proper, in excerpt.

## 4. How this changes the essay

Three consequences follow, pending the critic's pass.

First, cite the Barker veil file plus the Bradshaw temple file in the essay's veil and garment rows; cite Bokovoy alongside the meal and unity row (temple-discourse model, not meal theology); cite Hamblin as a hedge footnote.

Second, add a method note imitating the `ascending-to-presence-article` lens-not-fact discipline, so typology is marked as inference.

Third, add `forty-day/endowment/garment/meal` symbols to the index, and fix the README's 535/601 counts to 711.

## Limits and objections

Two limits are honest. First, this is a local-shelf survey only; until the published-literature pass is done, absence from the shelf is not absence from scholarship. Second, the eighty-percent estimate is the surveyor's judgment about an "ancient roots" brief, not a measurement; a critic may find the remaining twenty percent load-bearing rather than decorative.

## Conclusion

The shelf gives the essay its veil, priesthood, Eden, and garment legs to stand on, with method cover and a hedge close at hand — but it gives the forty-day argument nothing. Fetching that leg, and citing the legs it has with the discipline its neighbors model, is the next pass.
