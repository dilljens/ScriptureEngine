# Christ in the Old Testament: A Catalog of Him and His Atonement

## Abstract

This catalog argues that the Old Testament testifies of Christ throughout — not at isolated points but across Torah, Psalms and Wisdom, and the Prophets — and that each passage does so with a stated degree of certainty. The method is anchored typology: every entry carries a status (QUOTED, ALLUDED, or UNQUOTED), distinguishing what New Testament and latter-day writers fix to Christ from what echoes Him and what belongs to Him by content though never formally cited. The finding is that quotation fixes referents while silence leaves a further wing of texts — Davidic reign, Daniel's program, Zechariah's fountain, Micah's pardon — His by content and awaiting citation, on the pattern Luke 24 models.

## Introduction

The problem is how much of the Old Testament may rightly be read as Christ's. A maximal reading finds Him everywhere; a minimal reading admits only what the apostles quote. Neither satisfies the evidence on its own: beginning at Moses and all the prophets, He expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning Himself (:verse[luke.24.27]), a claim larger than the quotation record yet grounded in it.

Why it matters is that atonement language must stay His. If every reigning verb can layer across servants but atoning verbs transfer freely, substitution dissolves into metaphor. This catalog therefore separates **Savior-role** texts (atone, ransom, blot sin, mediate, resurrect), which stay Christ's in the act itself, from **King-role** texts (throne, rod, nations, reign), which can layer across Christ and Davidic servants: servants share only the applying (publish, witness, baptize, intercede), never the atoning act.

The roadmap is as follows. First, how to read the anchoring statuses; next, the voices this catalog argues with and why; then the catalog itself in four parts (Torah and temple, Psalms and Wisdom, Prophets, and special studies on the Angel, Jonah, and three restored psalm entries); and finally the texts not claimed, the limits of the method, and a closing reading rule.

## How to Read This Catalog

Versification follows the KJV tradition and the MT — the Masoretic Text, the standard Hebrew text — used across these papers; premodern composition dating is contested and not load-bearing here, since anchoring statuses rest on New Testament and latter-day citation, not on critical dates.

Each entry carries an anchoring status. **QUOTED** means a New Testament writer (or latter-day scripture) cites the passage of Christ directly: the referent is fixed. **ALLUDED** means the New Testament echoes it without formal citation: the referent is shown but not sealed. **UNQUOTED** means no New Testament writer cites it, yet its content (suffering Servant, atoning blood, pardoning God, rising Redeemer) belongs to no one but Him. Luke 24 claims more than the quotations show; silence is "not yet assigned," never "not His."

One more distinction runs through the whole: **Savior-role** texts (atone, ransom, blot sin, mediate, resurrect) stay Christ's in the act itself; servants share only the applying (publish, witness, baptize, intercede). **King-role** texts (throne, rod, nations, reign) can layer across Christ and Davidic servants. Atoning verbs are never transferable; reigning verbs often are.

## Voices Argued With

No catalog stands alone, so every voice cited here is introduced with what it contributes and why it matters to this argument.

**Luke, in the Emmaus account (:verse[luke.24.27]).** Who: the Gospel narrator reporting the risen Lord's exposition "in all the scriptures." What he found: that Moses and all the prophets concern Christ. Why he matters here: he supplies the method's charter — more is His than quotation alone shows — and the closing model (begin at Moses, pass through the prophets, find Him).

**Paul, in Romans, Corinthians, and the Pastorals.** Who: the apostle whose citations anchor pardon, resurrection, and mediation. What he argued: that :verse[psa.32.1-2] seals forgiveness (:verse[rom.4.7-8]), that Isaiah's feast grounds resurrection (:verse[1cor.15.54]), and that the one Mediator answers Job's lack (:verse[1tim.2.5-6] against :verse[job.9.33]). Why he matters here: his quotations fix referents, and his reapplications (as of :verse[isa.49.6] in :verse[acts.13.47]) set the participation precedent by which servant language extends without leaving Christ.

**The writer to the Hebrews.** Who: the expositor of priesthood and veil (traditionally ascribed to Paul; modern scholarship leaves authorship open, audience presumably Jewish-Christian). What he found: veil as flesh (:verse[heb.10.19-20]), heifer as lesser-to-greater (:verse[heb.9.13-14]), psalm as sacrificial charter (:verse[heb.10.5-7] on :verse[psa.40.6-8]). Why he matters here: he is the catalog's chief EXPOUNDER, turning quoted verses into whole doctrines of once-for-all sacrifice.

**John, in Gospel and Revelation.** Who: the witness of lifted brass, pierced side, and paschal bone. What he recorded: serpent lifted (:verse[john.3.14-15] on :verse[num.21.8-9]), bone unbroken (:verse[john.19.36] on :verse[exo.12.46]), zeal and thirst (:verse[john.2.17]; 19:28-29 on :verse[psa.69.9], 21). Why he matters here: his Passion narrative is the Psalter's quotation quarry in action.

**Matthew, Mark, Luke, and Acts.** Who: the narrators of fulfillment and the church's first exposition. What they argued: virgin birth (:verse[matt.1.23] on :verse[isa.7.14]), Galilean light (:verse[matt.4.15-16] on :verse[isa.9.1-2]), Servant gentleness (:verse[matt.12.17-21] on :verse[isa.42.1-4]), ascension gifts (:verse[eph.4.8] on :verse[psa.68.18]), and the council's use of Amos (:verse[acts.15.16-17] on :verse[amos.9.11-12]). Why they matter here: quotation fixes referents across birth, ministry, death, and church.

**Abinadi (Mosiah 14–15).** Who: the Nephite prophet-martyr expounding Isaiah 53 whole. What he argued: that the suffering Servant is God Himself come down. Why he matters here: he is the latter-day anchor for the catalog's central atonement text, joining the New Testament's scattered quotations into one exposition.

**Nephi, Moroni, and latter-day revelation (2 Nephi 21; :verse[jsh.1.41]; :verse[jsh.1.36-39]; :verse[dc113.113.1-6]).** Who: Restoration witnesses preserving and expounding Isaiah, Joel, and Malachi. What they found: Isaiah 11 preserved and expounded (2 Ne. 21; :verse[dc113.113.1-6]), Joel yet to be fulfilled as Moroni recited it (:verse[jsh.1.41] on :verse[joel.2.28-32]), Elijah identified (:verse[matt.11.14]; 17:12-13; :verse[jsh.1.36-39] on :verse[mal.4.5-6]). Why they matter here: they keep open, and partly fill, the unquoted wing Luke 24 leaves.

**Arius and Nicaea, on Proverbs 8.** Who: the fourth-century presbyter who weaponized "possessed me" (:verse[prov.8.22-31]) into creaturehood ("there was when he was not"), and the council of 325 that answered with homoousios — of one substance — without making this text Christological by decree. Why they matter here: they warn the catalog against supplying a quotation that is absent — echoes in :verse[john.1.3], :verse[col.1.16], :verse[heb.1.2] are real and noted, but ALLUDED at most.

## Christ in the Torah and the Temple

The books of Moses do not merely anticipate Christ at isolated points; they establish the ritual and narrative grammar by which His Atonement can afterward be understood. Lamb, blood, veil, mercy-seat, mediator: these are Torah words before they are creedal words.

**1. The seed of the woman (:verse[gen.3.15]).** To the serpent the Lord declares, "it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." What this says is that a single human offspring suffers a heel-wound while inflicting a mortal head-wound upon the serpent; what it means is a suffering yet victorious Redeemer. (*Zera'*, Hebrew for seed as offspring-line, is a collective noun per the standard lexica (BDB s.v.); the singular referent comes by later anchoring, not by Hebrew grammar alone.) *ALLUDED: :verse[rom.16.20]; UNQUOTED as formal citation.*

**2. The offering of Isaac (Gen. 22).** Abraham lays "the wood" upon Isaac (:verse[gen.22.6]), who asks "where is the lamb?" Abraham answers, "God will provide himself a lamb" (:verse[gen.22.7-8]). A ram is "offered up... in the stead of his son" (:verse[gen.22.13]), and the place is named Jehovah-jireh, "in the mount of the Lord it shall be seen" (:verse[gen.22.14]); Moriah, later the temple mount (:verse[2chr.3.1]). Father giving His Only Son, substitution by a provided victim, resurrection hope — that is the threefold point. *ALLUDED: :verse[heb.11.17-19] ("in a figure"); UNQUOTED as direct type.*

**3. The Passover lamb (Ex. 12; :verse[exo.12.46]).** A lamb "without blemish" (:verse[exo.12.5]), blood on the doorposts: "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (:verse[exo.12.13]), eaten in haste, "neither shall ye break a bone thereof" (:verse[exo.12.46]). Deliverance by substitutionary blood: the household lives because the lamb dies. *QUOTED: :verse[1cor.5.7]; :verse[john.19.36].*

From deliverance the Torah moves to the journey it makes possible. **4. Passage through the sea and the song (Ex. 14–15).** Through walled waters into covenant praise: "The Lord is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation" (:verse[exo.15.2]). Baptismal passage into sung salvation is the pattern. *ALLUDED: :verse[1cor.10.1-2] (baptized unto Moses); :verse[rev.15.3] (song of Moses and of the Lamb).*

**5. Bread and water in the wilderness (Ex. 16–17; Num. 20).** Manna as "bread from heaven" (:verse[exo.16.15]); water when the rock is smitten (:verse[exo.17.6]). Heavenly sustenance from unlikely, smitten sources. *QUOTED / ALLUDED: :verse[john.6.31], 35 ("I am the bread of life"); :verse[1cor.10.4] ("that Rock was Christ").*

**6. The serpent lifted up (:verse[num.21.8-9]).** The stung live by looking at brass on a pole. What this says is salvation by a lifted, cursed-form object through obedient gaze; what it means is that looking, not doing, saves. *QUOTED: :verse[john.3.14-15].*

**7. Atonement: mercy-seat, two goats, red heifer, refuge (Lev. 16; :verse[exo.25.17-22]; Num. 19; Num. 35).** Yearly blood "within the vail" (:verse[lev.16.15]); one goat slain, one bearing iniquities "unto a land not inhabited" (:verse[lev.16.22]); heifer ashes cleansing defilement (:verse[num.19.9]); the manslayer safe in refuge (:verse[num.35.25-28]). Four words summarize: propitiation, removal, purification, shelter. *QUOTED: :verse[rom.3.25] (propitiation); EXPOUNDED: :verse[heb.9.13-14] (heifer, lesser to greater); ALLUDED: :verse[heb.6.18] (fled for refuge).*

**8. The kinsman-redeemer (Lev. 25; Ruth; :verse[job.19.25]).** Near of kin redeems what his brother sold (:verse[lev.25.25]), as Boaz does (:verse[ruth.4.4-6]); "I know that my redeemer liveth" (:verse[job.19.25]). Redemption requires nearness of kin and full price — fulfilled, the catalog argues, when the Redeemer takes flesh to become kin. *UNQUOTED as such.*

**9. Vessels and veil (:verse[exo.26.31-33]; :verse[exo.25.30]; :verse[heb.9.4]).** Manna pot, budded rod, and tables within; veil dividing holy from most holy; showbread as presence-bread; perpetual lampstand light. Christ as true manna, living branch, covenant keeper, presence-bread and light, whose flesh opens what the veil closed. *QUOTED: :verse[heb.10.19-20] (veil, that is, His flesh); ALLUDED: :verse[john.6.35]; 8:12.*

**10. Moses the intercessor (:verse[exo.32.11-14], 30-32; :verse[num.14.19]).** "Blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book" (:verse[exo.32.32]). The servant-mediator who would bear judgment: the willingness is real, the efficacy only in Christ. *UNQUOTED; servant-intercessor type.*

**11. The prophet like Moses (:verse[deu.18.15-19]).** "A Prophet... like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken" (:verse[deu.18.15]). Mediator, lawgiver, deliverer in one office. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.3.22-23]; 7:37.*

The Torah supplies that vocabulary. The prophets spend it; the Gospels cash it.

## Christ in the Psalms and Wisdom

No portion of the Old Testament is more fully claimed by the New than the Psalter: language of the suffering Servant and titles of the exalted King, in words the apostles treat as Christ's own. Beside it, Wisdom bears quieter witness to the pre-mortal Christ and living Redeemer.

The Passion psalms come first because their anchoring is densest. **Psalm 22**: "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me... they pierced my hands and my feet... they part my garments" (:verse[psa.22.1], 16, 18). Forsakenness, piercing, and division in one psalm: the cross in David's mouth. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.27.46]; :verse[john.19.24].*

**Psalm 69:9, 21** (:verse[psa.69.9], 21): consuming zeal; gall and vinegar thirst. Zeal that devours and thirst that is mocked — temple-cleansing and cross together. *QUOTED: :verse[john.2.17]; 19:28-29.*

Three betrayal-and-death sayings follow in quick succession, each fixed by a Gospel mouth. **:verse[psa.41.9]**: familiar friend's heel lifted. *QUOTED: :verse[john.13.18].* **:verse[psa.31.5]**: committed spirit. *QUOTED: :verse[luke.23.46].* **:verse[psa.34.20]**: bones kept unbroken, the Paschal Lamb preserved. *ALLUDED: :verse[john.19.36]; the verse's own context is the righteous sufferer generally (Ps 34), while John's "a bone of him shall not be broken" cites Passover law first of all (:verse[exo.12.46]; :verse[num.9.12]).*

**:verse[psa.109.8]; 69:25**: office and habitation of the betrayer. The apostolic church reads its own vacancy — Judas's office — out of these verses. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.1.20].*

From suffering the catalog turns to enthronement, where the anchoring is equally firm. **:verse[psa.2.1-2], 7**: raging nations, begotten Son. Rage below, decree above. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.4.25-26]; :verse[heb.1.5]; 5:5.* **:verse[psa.45.6-7]**: "Thy throne, O God." Addressed deity enthroned forever. *QUOTED: :verse[heb.1.8-9].* **:verse[psa.110.1], 4**: right hand; priest after Melchizedek. The most cited psalm in the New Testament — session and priesthood in two verses. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.22.44]; :verse[acts.2.34-35]; :verse[heb.5.6]; 7:17, 21.*

Resurrection, offering, ascent, cornerstone, dominion, and pardon close the quoted psalm sequence. **:verse[psa.16.10]**: no corruption for the Holy One. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.2.25-28]; 13:35.* **:verse[psa.40.6-8]**: "a body hast thou prepared me... Lo, I come to do thy will." *QUOTED: :verse[heb.10.5-7]*, charter of the once-for-all sacrifice. **:verse[psa.68.18]**: ascended, led captivity, gave gifts. *QUOTED: :verse[eph.4.8].* **:verse[psa.118.22]**: rejected stone, head of corner. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.21.42]; :verse[acts.4.11]; :verse[1pet.2.7].* **:verse[psa.8.4-6]**: dominion restored under man's feet. *QUOTED: :verse[1cor.15.27]; :verse[heb.2.6-8].* **:verse[psa.32.1-2]**: blessed forgiven, sin covered. The one pardon psalm anchored. *QUOTED: :verse[rom.4.7-8].*

Unquoted but plain, on covenant and pardon: **Psalm 72** (king's son, sea-to-sea dominion, poor delivered); **:verse[psa.89.3], 27-29** (covenant, Firstborn highest, throne forever); **:verse[psa.132.11-14]** (fruit of body enthroned; rest forever, dwelling); **:verse[psa.21.4]** (length of days forever, resurrection in a line); **:verse[psa.51.10]** (create clean heart); **:verse[psa.103.3-4], 12** (forgives, heals, redeems; east-from-west removal). A literal, everlasting, embodied kingship through David's line, still awaiting full citation.

Wisdom's witness is quieter and wholly unquoted: **:verse[prov.8.22-31]** (possessed Wisdom, master workman; echoed :verse[john.1.3]; :verse[col.1.16]; :verse[heb.1.2], never quoted); **:verse[job.19.25-27]** (living latter-day embodied Redeemer, never quoted); **:verse[job.33.23-24]** (messenger-interpreter finds ransom, never quoted); **:verse[job.9.33]** (no daysman, answered :verse[1tim.2.5-6] without quotation).

The Psalter is the Gospels' quotation quarry for the Passion and the priesthood. The Davidic-covenant wing (72; 89; 132) and Job's Redeemer stand plain but as yet unquoted.

## Christ in the Prophets

The prophets see Him before He comes, naming His work with unusual precision (person, ministry, suffering, pardon, return), and the New Testament with the Restoration fixes many, though not all, of these texts to Jesus Christ.

Isaiah opens with birth and light. **:verse[isa.7.14]**: virgin/Immanuel. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.1.23]*, with dual-fulfillment layer (a child in Isaiah's day foreshadowing the holy birth). **:verse[isa.9.1-2]**: Galilean light. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.4.15-16].* The throne-names (9:6–7) echoed :verse[luke.1.32-33], UNQUOTED as such. **:verse[isa.11.1-10]**: Branch, Spirit, universal rest. *QUOTED: :verse[rom.15.12] (11:10); expounded :verse[dc113.113.1-6]; preserved 2 Ne. 21.*

Feast, forerunner, and Servant follow. **:verse[isa.25.6-8]**: feast, death swallowed, tears wiped. *QUOTED: :verse[1cor.15.54]; ALLUDED: :verse[rev.21.4].* **:verse[isa.40.3]**: prepare the way. *QUOTED in all four Gospels (:verse[matt.3.3]; :verse[mark.1.3]; :verse[luke.3.4]; :verse[john.1.23]): of John*, marking a forerunner layer. **:verse[isa.42.1-4]**: bruised reed unbroken. *QUOTED at length: :verse[matt.12.17-21].*

The Servant's reach extends without leaving Him. **:verse[isa.49.1-13]**: shaft hidden, light to Gentiles (49:6). *QUOTED: :verse[acts.13.47] reapplies :verse[isa.49.6] to Paul (participation precedent); :verse[2cor.6.2] uses :verse[isa.49.8].* Servant language extends to His servants without leaving Him.

Suffering and invitation stand side by side. **:verse[isa.50.4-11]**: wakened ear, back to smiters, flint face. *UNQUOTED in the New Testament, yet His by content.* **:verse[isa.52.13-53]:12**: exalted yet marred (52:13–14), despised, substituted (53:5), justifying many (53:11), seed and intercession (53:10–12). *QUOTED repeatedly: :verse[john.12.38] (53:1); :verse[acts.8.32-35] (53:7-8); :verse[matt.8.17] (53:4); :verse[luke.22.37] (53:12); :verse[1pet.2.22-24], and expounded whole by Abinadi (Mosiah 14-15) as God Himself come down.* **:verse[isa.55.1-3], 6-7**: free waters, seek while near, abundant pardon. *UNQUOTED as such.*

Liberty, presence, and covenant complete Isaiah. **:verse[isa.61.1-3]**: liberty, bound-up hearts. *QUOTED: :verse[luke.4.18-19], stopping mid-verse before vengeance: first coming claimed, second reserved.* **:verse[isa.63.8-9]**: "He was their Saviour... the angel of his presence saved them." *UNQUOTED: veiled angel-Christology of presence.*

Jeremiah and Ezekiel carry Branch, covenant, and heart. **:verse[jer.23.5-6]; 33:15-16**: Righteous Branch, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS. *UNQUOTED.* **:verse[jer.31.31-34]**: new covenant, sins remembered no more. *QUOTED: :verse[heb.8.8-12].* **:verse[ezek.34.23-24]; 37:24-25**: David-servant shepherd-king. *UNQUOTED.* **:verse[ezek.36.25-27]**: clean water, new heart and spirit. *ALLUDED: :verse[john.3.5].*

Daniel gives program and person. **:verse[dan.7.13-14]**: Son of Man receiving dominion. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.26.64].* The saints' share (7:18, 27), UNQUOTED. **:verse[dan.9.24-27]**: finish transgression, reconciliation, anointing; "cut off, but not for himself." *UNQUOTED: the atonement's program verse.*

The Twelve supply ransom, Spirit, restoration, birthplace, pardon, price, piercing, refining, and sign — each unpacked rather than listed. **:verse[hos.6.1-3]**: torn yet healed, raised the third day: a corporate-resurrection text ("raise *us* up"), not an individual-messiah prediction, and Hos 6:4 immediately rebukes the repentance as a morning cloud. Any link to the "scriptures" of :verse[1cor.15.4] is inference at most. *UNQUOTED as resurrection prophecy.* **:verse[hos.11.1]**: Son called from Egypt. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.2.15].* **:verse[hos.13.4]**: no saviour beside Me. *UNQUOTED.* **:verse[hos.13.14]**: ransom from grave. *Used: :verse[1cor.15.55].*

**Joel, Amos, Micah, and Zechariah–Malachi** continue the sequence. **:verse[joel.2.28-32]**: poured Spirit, deliverance. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.2.17-21]; recited by Moroni (:verse[jsh.1.41]) as yet to be fulfilled.* **:verse[amos.9.11-12]**: fallen tabernacle of David raised. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.15.16-17].* **:verse[mic.5.2]**: Bethlehem Ruler. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.2.6].* **:verse[mic.7.18-19]**: delights in mercy, casts sins to sea depths. *UNQUOTED: pure pardon.*

**:verse[zech.9.9]**: lowly King on an ass. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.21.5]; :verse[john.12.15].* **:verse[zech.11.12-13]**: thirty pieces to the potter. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.27.9-10] (ascribed Jeremiah).* **:verse[zech.12.10]**: pierced. *QUOTED: :verse[john.19.37]; :verse[rev.1.7].* **:verse[zech.13.1]**: fountain for sin. *UNQUOTED.* **:verse[zech.13.6-7]**: wounds, smitten shepherd. *13:7 QUOTED: :verse[matt.26.31]; 13:6 ("wounds in thine hands") UNQUOTED — inference, not citation.*

**:verse[mal.3.1]**: messenger preparing the way. *QUOTED: :verse[mark.1.2].* **:verse[mal.3.2-3]**: Refiner purifying Levi. *UNQUOTED.* **:verse[mal.4.5-6]**: Elijah. *Identified: :verse[matt.11.14]; 17:12-13; :verse[jsh.1.36-39].*

The minor remainder: **:verse[hab.1.5]**: work disbelieved. *QUOTED: :verse[acts.13.41].* **:verse[jonah.1.17]**: three days. *QUOTED: :verse[matt.12.40].* **:verse[gen.49.10]** (Shiloh) and **:verse[num.24.17]** (star): *ALLUDED (:verse[matt.2.2]; :verse[rev.22.16]), UNQUOTED.*

Quotation fixes referents; allusion extends them (49:6 to Paul, 55:3 to :verse[acts.13.34]); silence leaves the Davidic-covenant wing, Daniel 9, Zechariah 3/6/13:1, Micah 7, and :verse[hos.13.4] open: His by content, awaiting citation.

## The Angel of the LORD

A figure distinct from the Lord who speaks as the Lord in first person: to Hagar, "I will multiply thy seed exceedingly" (:verse[gen.16.10]), a promise only God makes, from a mouth called Angel; staying Abraham's knife, "seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me" (:verse[gen.22.11-12]): *from me*, yet the speaker is introduced as Angel; at the bush, "I am the God of thy father" (:verse[exo.3.6]), and Moses hides his face "for he was afraid to look upon God"; to Manoah, a name "secret" (that is, Wonderful) (:verse[judg.13.18]), ascending in the altar flame. What this says is a sent Speaker bearing the divine Name; what it means is theophany — one who both is sent and speaks as Sender. *UNQUOTED throughout as formal citation; the reasoning is theophanic: one who both is sent and speaks as Sender.* The catalog claims a pattern (sent Speaker bearing the Name), not a verse the apostles pinned.

## Jonah, Deepened

The catalog's Jonah entry (:verse[jonah.1.17]; :verse[matt.12.40]) states the sign; the rest of the book extends it. Nineveh repents at preaching and is spared (Jonah 3): *QUOTED in application: "The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment... because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here" (:verse[matt.12.41]).* What this says is Gentile repentance at a lesser preaching; what it means is indictment — Gentile sailors and Ninevites respond while the prophet sulks over a gourd. Typology with an edge: the sign authenticates, the response indicts.

## Psalms: Three Quoted Verses the Catalog Missed

Three quoted psalm verses belong in the catalog and were missing, and each is unpacked because quotation fixes the referent.

**Psalm 69:4**: "They that hate me without a cause are more than the hairs of mine head" (:verse[psa.69.4]). *QUOTED: "They hated me without a cause" (:verse[john.15.25]).* What this says is causeless hatred; what it means is messianic credential, fixed by the Lord's own mouth.

**Psalm 69:9b**: "The reproaches of them that reproached thee fell on me" (with the zeal of :verse[psa.69.9] already entered above). *QUOTED: "even Christ pleased not himself; but, as it is written, The reproaches of them that reproached thee fell on me" (:verse[rom.15.3]).* What Paul's use says is that the psalm's reproach-bearing is Christ's ethic and example; why it matters is that suffering here becomes pattern for His people without ceasing to be His.

**Psalm 22:22**: "I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee" (:verse[psa.22.22]). *QUOTED: "I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee" (:verse[heb.2.12]).* The psalm does not end at the cross: forsaken (v1), pierced (v16), divided garments (v18), then vindicated praise among brethren. Hebrews plants the risen Christ inside the congregation singing.

## Appendix: Texts Not Claimed

Honesty requires the negative catalog, because a method that claims everything proves nothing.

**Esther** names neither God nor Christ; no entry is forced: providence without citation stays outside. What this says about method is that absence of divine naming plus absence of anchoring equals restraint. **Song of Songs** is claimed by some as Christ-and-church allegory; the essay declines: with no quotation and no unmistakable content, allegorical reading is devotion, not anchoring. **Proverbs 8** (Wisdom possessed, master workman, entered above at :verse[prov.8.22-31]) stays ALLUDED at most: Arius weaponized "possessed me" into creaturehood, Nicea answered without making the text Christological by decree. Echoes in :verse[john.1.3], :verse[col.1.16], :verse[heb.1.2] are real and noted; quotation is absent and the essay will not supply it. What is not shown is not denied, only not catalogued.

## Limits and Objections

Three bounds keep this catalog honest. First, collective nouns and corporate texts limit what grammar alone proves: *zera'* is collective, so :verse[gen.3.15] needs later anchoring for its singular referent, and :verse[hos.6.1-3] is corporate ("raise us up") under immediate rebuke (Hos 6:4), so any link to :verse[1cor.15.4] is inference at most. Second, layering runs one way: King-role verbs may be shared with Davidic servants, but Savior-role verbs (atone, ransom, blot sin) are never transferable, and servant texts (Moses' "blot me") remain types precisely because they fail to atone. Third, allusion extends but does not seal: :verse[isa.49.6] reapplies to Paul, :verse[psa.34.20] rests first on Passover law, and the Angel pattern is claimed as pattern, not as a verse the apostles pinned.

## Conclusion

Never use an unquoted text to displace a quoted one; never let quoted texts close the canon of what may yet be cited. The apostles quoted what their hour required and left the rest (Davidic reign, Daniel's program, Zechariah's fountain, Micah's sea-depth pardon) for latter-day witnesses (Abinadi, Nephi, Moroni, D&C 113) and for readers willing to do what Luke 24 models: begin at Moses, pass through all the prophets, and find Him.
