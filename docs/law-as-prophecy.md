# The Law as Prophecy: Feasts, Sacrifices, and Sabbaths Foretelling Christ

## Abstract

This essay argues that the law of Moses functions as prophecy: its dated feasts rehearse the comings of Christ, its sacrifices enact His death, and its Sabbaths promise His rest. The method is typological catalog — each institution is set beside its New Testament fulfillment or announced future, with the strength of the link labeled (quoted, alluded, expounded, or unquoted pattern). The finding is that the spring feasts were fulfilled in order at His first coming while the fall feasts stand as His return calendar, and that sacrifice, purity, tabernacle, and social law alike encode substitution, fellowship, and release through Him.

## Introduction

Does the law of Moses merely regulate Israel, or does it predict Christ? The question matters because the New Testament itself claims the predictive reading: the law held "a shadow of good things to come" (:verse[heb.10.1]), "the example and shadow of heavenly things" (:verse[heb.8.5]), given as "schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ" (:verse[gal.3.24]), described in latter-day scripture as "a type of his coming" with "types, and shadows" shown concerning Him (:verse[alma.25.15]; :verse[mosiah.3.15]).

This companion to "Christ in the Old Testament" tests that claim across the whole Mosaic system rather than isolated proof-texts. It proceeds in four movements: first the seven dated feasts, then sacrifices and purity laws as acted prediction, then tabernacle furniture, priestly garments, and the Day of Atonement, and finally Sabbaths, Jubilee, and social laws. Throughout, each type is paired with what fulfills it, what remains future, and how firmly the connection is anchored. Versification follows the KJV tradition and the MT — the Masoretic Text, the standard Hebrew text — used across these papers; Torah composition debates are not load-bearing here, since the argument runs from the New Testament's own typological claims rather than from critical dating.

## Context: How This Essay Reads Types

The essay uses a consistent vocabulary for evidential weight, and the reader needs it before the catalog begins. **QUOTED** means a New Testament or latter-day text cites the passage of Christ directly. **ALLUDED** means the later text echoes it without formal citation. **EXPOUNDED** means an extended doctrinal exposition (chiefly Hebrews) builds on it. **UNQUOTED** means no later writer cites it, yet its content — substitution, bearing, release — belongs to the same pattern.

Two interpretive traditions also appear and must not be passed over as bare names. The **Sadducees**, a Second Temple priestly party, read "the morrow after the Sabbath" in the Firstfruits statute as the weekly Sabbath, which fixes Firstfruits to Sunday; what they argued matters here because the essay's day-precision for the spring feasts follows that Sunday reading. The **Pharisees**, their rival party of scribal interpreters, read "Sabbath" as Nisan 15, which fixes Firstfruits to Nisan 16; what they argued matters here as the live alternative the essay notes without adjudicating. The **Mishnah**, the early rabbinic codification of oral custom (compiled c. 200 CE under Judah ha-Nasi), matters where the essay flags the "four cups" alignment as Mishnah-era custom rather than Torah text — a guardrail against presenting later tradition as Mosaic prophecy.

## The Seven Feasts as Dated Prophecy

The governing claim of this section is simple: the feasts rehearse His comings on set dates — spring at His first coming, fall at His return.

**Passover (Nisan 14)** opens the year because deliverance opens the story. "In the fourteenth day of the first month at even is the LORD's passover" (:verse[lev.23.5]; :verse[exo.12.1-14]). The details are exacting: a first-year male lamb without blemish, kept four days, slain at even, blood on lintel and posts, flesh roast in haste, and "neither shall ye break a bone thereof" (:verse[exo.12.46]; :verse[num.9.12]). What this says is substitution by unblemished blood; what it means is that the household lives because another dies; why it supports the point is that the New Testament fixes the referent — *QUOTED: "Christ our passover is sacrificed for us" (:verse[1cor.5.7]); "A bone of him shall not be broken" (:verse[john.19.36]).* Nothing future remains in the type; its memorial continues transformed in the sacrament (:verse[matt.26.27-28]; :verse[luke.22.19-20]).

**Unleavened Bread (Nisan 15–21)** follows Passover because consecration follows redemption. Seven days, leaven removed, "bread of affliction" eaten in haste (:verse[lev.23.6-8]; :verse[exo.12.15-20]; :verse[deu.16.3]). *ALLUDED: "let us keep the feast, not with old leaven... but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (:verse[1cor.5.8]).* The unpacking is direct: Christ the sinless one is the unleavened loaf, and His people purge sin after accepting the Lamb.

**Firstfruits (morrow after the Sabbath)** turns harvest into resurrection promise. The waved sheaf consecrates the harvest, none eaten until offered (:verse[lev.23.9-14]). *QUOTED conceptually: "now is Christ risen... the firstfruits of them that slept... Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ's at his coming" (:verse[1cor.15.20], :verse[1cor.15.23]).* What remains is the general resurrection (:verse[1cor.15.52-54]; :verse[dc88.88.96-98]).

**Weeks/Pentecost (fifty days later)** gathers the harvest publicly. Two leavened wave loaves, full public gathering (:verse[lev.23.15-21]; :verse[deu.16.9-12]). *QUOTED for the event: the Spirit poured out with tongues of fire (:verse[acts.2.1-4]), "this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel" (:verse[acts.2.16-17]; :verse[joel.2.28-32]).* The timing and the outpouring are text; the loaves themselves are honestly puzzling — either the church still imperfect yet accepted, or Jew and Gentile in one harvest (:verse[acts.2.5-11]) — and the essay marks that reading as inference, not text.

From spring the calendar turns to fall, and from fulfilled to largely future.

**Trumpets (Tishri 1)** is blasting, rest, holy convocation (:verse[lev.23.23-25]; :verse[num.29.1-6]). *ALLUDED, principally future: gathering "with a great sound of a trumpet" (:verse[matt.24.31]); "at the last trump... the dead shall be raised" (:verse[1cor.15.52]); "the trump of God" (:verse[1thes.4.16]); seven trumpet judgments (Rev. 8-11).* Almost all remains: the awakening blast of return.

**Day of Atonement (Tishri 10)** centers the fall. High priest alone within the veil with blood; two goats, one slain, one bearing iniquities away; the people afflict souls (:verse[lev.23.26-32]; Lev. 16). *EXPOUNDED: "by his own blood he entered in once... having obtained eternal redemption" (:verse[heb.9.11-12]); "by one offering he hath perfected for ever" (:verse[heb.10.14]).* A national layer remains: Israel looking on the pierced One with "a fountain opened... for sin and for uncleanness" (:verse[zech.12.10]; :verse[zech.13.1]).

**Tabernacles (Tishri 15–22)** closes the year in rejoicing: booths, rejoicing, eighth-day assembly (:verse[lev.23.33-43]; :verse[neh.8.14-17]). *ALLUDED via feast background: "If any man thirst, let him come unto me" on the great day (:verse[john.7.37-38], feast named :verse[john.7.2]); "I am the light of the world" amid its lights (:verse[john.8.12]).* What remains is all nations keeping Tabernacles in the kingdom (:verse[zech.14.16-19]).

The section's summary therefore holds: spring feasts were fulfilled in order (day-precision per §1's Sunday reading; the calendar caveats stand as stated in Limits); fall feasts stand as His return calendar.

## Sacrifices and Purity as Acted Prediction

If the feasts date His comings, the sacrifices enact His death: each sacrifice enacts substitution, each purity law enacts separation followed by return, and each surplus detail carries meaning.

The **burnt offering** — male without blemish, wholly consumed, "a sweet savour" (:verse[lev.1.9], :verse[lev.1.13], :verse[lev.1.17]) — says total consecration. That is, nothing is held back. *QUOTED concept: "an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour" (:verse[eph.5.2]).*

The **peace offering** — God the fat, priest breast and shoulder, offerer eating before the Lord (Lev. 3; :verse[lev.7.11-18]) — says fellowship follows atonement. The essay is careful here: this is a communion analogy (inference, not Lev 3's own doctrine), not Lev 3's own doctrine. *ALLUDED: "we being many are one bread" (:verse[1cor.10.16-18]), which argues Lord's Supper versus idol-feasts rather than expounding Leviticus; the risen Lord's bread and wine "in remembrance of me" (:verse[3ne.18.7], 11).*

The **sin offering**, for unwitting sins with blood applied by status — nearer God for greater guilt (:verse[lev.4.2], :verse[lev.4.13], :verse[lev.4.22], :verse[lev.4.27]) — says accountability is graded. *ALLUDED: degrees of stripes by knowledge (:verse[luke.12.47-48]); "where there is no law... no punishment," mercies delivering the lawless (:verse[2ne.9.25-26]; :verse[alma.42.17-21]).*

The **trespass offering** — confession, unblemished ram, principal plus a fifth restored (:verse[lev.5.5-6], :verse[lev.5.14-16]; :verse[lev.6.4-7]) — says forgiveness funds restitution. *ALLUDED in spirit: Zacchaeus restoring fourfold, salvation coming to his house (:verse[luke.19.8-9]).*

From offerings the essay moves to purifications, where the same substitution logic appears in stranger dress. The **red heifer** — spotless, slain without the camp, burned with cedar, hyssop, scarlet; ashes for corpse-defilement; the clean preparer made unclean (:verse[num.19.2-9]) — says death cleanses death outside camp. *QUOTED and EXPOUNDED: "how much more shall the blood of Christ... purge your conscience" (:verse[heb.9.13-14]); suffered "without the gate" (:verse[heb.13.12]).*

The **leper's cleansing** — two birds, cedar, scarlet, hyssop; one slain over living water, one freed; washing, shaving, eighth-day lambs with oil on ear, thumb, toe (:verse[lev.14.4-10], :verse[lev.14.14], :verse[lev.14.17]) — says death and flight, blood and water, anointed hearing, doing, walking, with the eighth day as new beginning. *QUOTED procedure: "shew thyself to the priest, and offer the gift that Moses commanded" (:verse[matt.8.2-4]).*

**Childbirth and circumcision** — eighth-day cutting (:verse[lev.12.2-3]; :verse[gen.17.12]); the poor woman's "two turtles, or two young pigeons" (:verse[lev.12.8]) — locates the holy family inside the law's poverty provision. *QUOTED narrative: Mary's offering (:verse[luke.2.24]); the child circumcised and named JESUS (:verse[luke.2.21]).* Baptism as "circumcision made without hands" (:verse[col.2.11-12]) carries the eighth-day sign forward.

**Issues of blood** — prolonged issue separates, touch transmits (:verse[lev.15.25-27], :verse[lev.15.31]) — set up a *narrative fulfillment, UNQUOTED: twelve years' issue healed by a hem-touch, virtue going out instead of defilement coming in: "daughter... go in peace" (:verse[mark.5.25-34]).* The direction of contagion reverses.

**Foods** — daily discipline of holiness (:verse[lev.11.44-45]; :verse[deu.14.3-21]) — become the Gentile question. *QUOTED event: "What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common" (:verse[acts.10.11-15]), governing Gentile admission (:verse[acts.10.28], :verse[acts.10.34-35]); foods not defiling the heart (:verse[mark.7.18-19]); ordinances "a shadow of things to come" (:verse[col.2.16-17]).*

**Tassels with blue cord** — fringes "that ye may remember all the commandments" (:verse[num.15.37-41]) — give the bleeding woman's touch its point. *Narrative, exact term UNQUOTED (inference from the shared term): the bleeding woman touches the kraspedon, the hem-fringe (:verse[matt.9.20]; :verse[matt.14.36]): she grasps remembrance itself.*

**Cities of refuge** — six cities, trial, safety till the high priest dies (:verse[num.35.9-15], :verse[num.35.25-28]; Josh. 20) — say freedom comes through another's death. *ALLUDED: "fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope" (:verse[heb.6.18]).*

## Tabernacle Furniture, Priestly Garments, and the Day

From acted rites the essay moves to the house where they happen: the house holds His presence, the garments bear His people, and the Day enacts removal.

The **lampstand** — beaten pure gold, seven lamps giving light "over against it" (:verse[exo.25.31-37]) — says light is positioned, not ambient: illumination has a direction. *ALLUDED: "I am the light of the world" (:verse[john.8.12]); the seven churches as lampstands (:verse[rev.1.20]).* The stand's work is holding light where God points it; inference beyond that is the essay's own, flagged as such.

The **table of shewbread** — bread "before me alway" (:verse[exo.25.30]) — says fellowship is perpetual, twelve tribes faced across it. *ALLUDED: "I am the living bread which came down from heaven" (:verse[john.6.51]); the table's constancy, not its menu, is the text's point.*

The **altar of incense** — Aaron burns sweet incense morning and evening when he dresses the lamps (:verse[exo.30.7-8]) — says prayer is timed to tending: intercession and illumination serviced together. *QUOTED in symbol: golden vials "which are the prayers of saints" (:verse[rev.5.8]); incense offered "with the prayers of all saints" (:verse[rev.8.3-4]).*

The **veil and ark** — the veil hangs to bring the ark within (:verse[exo.26.33]); above the mercy seat, between cherubim, "there I will meet with thee" (:verse[exo.25.22]) — say meeting has an address. *QUOTED event: the veil rent top to bottom at the death (:verse[matt.27.51]); EXPOUNDED: the way consecrated "through the veil, that is to say, his flesh" (:verse[heb.10.19-20]).* What the veil concealed, the body opened.

The **ephod and memorial stones** — onyx stones graven with tribal names set on the shoulders "for stones of memorial" (:verse[exo.28.9-12]) — say bearing precedes blessing: the people ride into presence on strength not their own. *UNQUOTED as statute; fulfilled in pattern: the Servant bearing, the Shepherd carrying.*

The **breastpiece, Urim and Thummim** — judgment worn "upon Aaron's heart, when he goeth in" (:verse[exo.28.29-30]) — say decision and affection are co-located: verdicts carried where love lives. Urim means lights and Thummim means perfections; the mechanism is unexplained in the text, so no mechanics are inferred here.

The **bells and pomegranates** — golden bells alternating pomegranates on the robe's hem, "his sound shall be heard when he goeth in... and when he cometh out, that he die not" (:verse[exo.28.33-35]) — say mediation has life at stake, with coming and going announced. *UNQUOTED; the pattern (a heard entry securing life) belongs to the one Mediator.*

The **mitre plate** — pure gold graven "HOLINESS TO THE LORD" (:verse[exo.28.36-38]), borne on the forehead to "bear the iniquity of the holy things" — says consecration is worn facing forward, and even Israel's offerings need a bearer. "For glory and for beauty" (:verse[exo.28.2]) governs the whole wardrobe: atonement dressed, not merely done.

**The Day of Atonement, in full**, gathers the feasts section's summary into enacted detail. Two goats, one lot: lots cast, "one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat" (:verse[lev.16.8]). The slain goat's blood goes within the veil, sprinkled on and before the mercy seat (:verse[lev.16.14-15]): *EXPOUNDED: the high priest enters "alone once every year, not without blood" (:verse[heb.9.7]), but Christ "by his own blood... entered in once... having obtained eternal redemption" (:verse[heb.9.11-12]).* Then the live goat: both hands laid on, "all the iniquities... and all their transgressions in all their sins" confessed over it, sent "by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness" (:verse[lev.16.21-22]): sin both covered (blood) and carried away (goat). *ALLUDED: "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (:verse[john.1.29]).* The people afflict their souls while it happens, and "on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you" (:verse[lev.16.29-30]). On Azazel, the Hebrew leaves open scapegoat-rendering versus proper-name reading; the essay claims only what both readings share: removal, not merely covering. The national layer remains future (:verse[zech.12.10]; :verse[zech.13.1]).

## Sabbaths, Jubilee, and Social Laws

The final movement widens from sanctuary to society: Sabbath promises His rest, Jubilee His release, and the social laws His redemption logic.

The **weekly Sabbath** joins creation's rest (:verse[exo.20.8-11]) and exodus rest (:verse[deu.5.12-15]) in one day. *EXPOUNDED: "there remaineth therefore a rest" (:verse[heb.4.1-11], quoting :verse[psa.95.7-11]); "The sabbath was made for man... the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath" (:verse[mark.2.27-28]); shadow, body Christ's (:verse[col.2.16-17]).*

The **sabbatical year** — land rests, debts released, slaves freed (:verse[exo.23.10-11]; :verse[lev.25.1-7]; :verse[deu.15.1-18]) — says bondage is temporary under covenant; broken release was judged (:verse[neh.5.1-13]; :verse[jer.34.8-22]). *UNQUOTED directly; enacted in history.*

The **Jubilee fiftieth** — trumpet on Atonement Day, liberty proclaimed, every man to his possession (:verse[lev.25.8-17]) — is claimed by Christ's own reading. *QUOTED: "to preach deliverance... to set at liberty... the acceptable year of the Lord" (:verse[luke.4.18-19], stopping before vengeance).* No Jubilee ordinance appears in the D&C.

**Firstborn redemption** — consecrated, redeemed by price or lamb (:verse[exo.13.2], :verse[exo.13.12-15]; :verse[num.3.40-51]; :verse[num.18.15-16]) — points to the Firstborn Son. *QUOTED: "bringeth in the firstbegotten" (:verse[heb.1.6]); ALLUDED: "firstborn of every creature... firstborn from the dead" (:verse[col.1.15-18]); "first begotten of the dead" (:verse[rev.1.5]); "church of the firstborn" (:verse[heb.12.23]).*

The **kinsman-redeemer** — near kin pays, reclaims land, frees brother (:verse[lev.25.23-28], :verse[lev.25.47-55]); Ruth enacts, Jeremiah performs as sign mid-siege (Ruth 3-4; :verse[jer.32.7-9]) — teaches incarnational logic: nearness qualifies. *UNQUOTED as statute; fulfilled in pattern (inference, not the statute's own doctrine).*

The **levirate** — raising seed to the dead brother (:verse[deu.25.5-10]; Gen. 38; Ruth) — becomes a resurrection text in dispute. *ALLUDED: the Sadducees' seven-brother trap answered from resurrection: God of the living (:verse[matt.22.23-33]).*

**Witnesses** — established by two or three mouths (:verse[deu.17.6]; 19:15) — govern testimony itself. *QUOTED repeatedly: :verse[matt.18.16]; :verse[john.8.17]; :verse[2cor.13.1]; :verse[heb.10.28], and preserved precisely: "by the words of three... I will establish my word" (:verse[2ne.11.3]); "In the mouth of two or three witnesses" (:verse[dc6.6.28]); "in the mouth of three witnesses" of the plates (:verse[ether.5.4]).*

The **divorce writ** — bill for uncleanness, no return after remarriage (:verse[deu.24.1-4]) — frames the covenant dispute. *QUOTED in dispute: "from the beginning it was not so," hardness of hearts (:verse[matt.19.3-9]).* Covenant infidelity and restoration run through Hosea 1-3, :verse[jer.3.8], :verse[isa.50.1] (preserved :verse[2ne.7.1]).

**Gleaning** — corners and sheaves left for poor and stranger (:verse[lev.19.9-10]; :verse[deu.24.19-22]); Ruth 2 narrates it — sets the Sabbath controversy. *ALLUDED in setting, QUOTED :verse[hos.6.6]: plucking grain on Sabbath defended as Lord of harvest: "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice" (:verse[matt.12.1-8]).*

Finally, **Sabbath healings as acted Jubilee** — grainfields, withered hand (:verse[matt.12.1-13]), Bethesda's thirty-eight years (:verse[john.5.1-18]), born-blind washing sighted (John 9): seven acts of loosing on rest days, "loosed from thine infirmity" (:verse[luke.13.12]) — show bond loosened on rest day: Jubilee in miniature. *UNQUOTED as a Jubilee claim; the "seven acts" framing is the essay's own inference, not the text's count.*

## Limits and Objections

Three limits keep this reading honest. First, calendar precision is tradition-dependent: the Sunday reading of Firstfruits follows the Sadducees against the Pharisees, and Gospel chronology itself is debated, so fulfilled "on the days" claims the pattern, not a settled calendar proof. Second, inference is marked where the text stops: the two leavened Pentecost loaves, the lampstand's further symbolism, and the Urim mechanism are all flagged as interpretation rather than text. Third, later custom is not Torah: the "four cups" alignment belongs to Mishnah-era tradition and cannot bear prophetic weight, and the essay's communion analogies (peace offering, table) are analogies, not the passages' own doctrine.

## Conclusion

Calendar and courtroom testify weekly and fiftieth-yearly: "schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ" (:verse[gal.3.24]). Feasts date His comings, sacrifices enact His death, furniture and garments display His mediation, and Sabbaths and social laws rehearse His rest and release.

## Works Argued With

- The Pentateuchal feasts, offerings, purity, tabernacle, and social statutes (Leviticus, Exodus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Genesis), with latter-day exposition in Hebrews, the Gospels, Acts, Epistles, and Revelation as cited above.
- Sadducees and Pharisees on the dating of Firstfruits; Mishnah-era Passover custom (four cups), cited only to exclude it from Torah-level claims.
